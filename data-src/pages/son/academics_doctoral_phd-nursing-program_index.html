Application deadline

Jan. 15

Applications accepted for fall entry only. Applications received after this deadline will be reviewed on a case-by-case basis.

The PhD in Nursing & Health Science program at the University of Rochester sets itself apart by offering close mentorship from nationally-recognized faculty. Our collaborative learning environment is enriched by interaction with researchers across many disciplines, allowing you to learn how to make meaningful discoveries and innovations in a changing healthcare landscape.

As part of University of Rochester Medicine, the School of Nursing engages in groundbreaking interdisciplinary research through collaborative partnerships with nurses and other healthcare practitioners across the campus, region, and nation.

Program pathways

At the University of Rochester School of Nursing, we offer two flexible pathways to the PhD in Nursing & Health Science. Whether you’re continuing your studies after a master’s degree or ready to begin doctoral work directly after your BS, you’ll find a rigorous curriculum that adapts to your goals—supported by close mentorship to help you launch your career as a nurse scientist.

The traditional post-MS pathway provides a streamlined transition from master’s-level study to doctoral research. Building on your existing graduate training, advance directly into specialized PhD coursework and scholarly inquiry. With a strong theoretical and methodological foundation already in place, you’re well positioned to refine your research focus and contribute to the science of nursing and health science from the earliest stages of the program.

The post-BS pathway accelerates your transition toward becoming the healthcare researcher you aspire to be, allowing you to immerse yourself in research earlier in your career. By bypassing a separate master’s program, you maintain momentum in your academic journey and begin building the skills needed to lead innovative nursing research from your very first semester.

Whichever path you choose, you’ll join a collaborative community and prepare to launch a career at the forefront of nursing and health science research. Explore the pathways below to determine which best fits your background, interests, and schedule.

Highlights
  • Receive tuition waiver for required coursework credit, competitive stipend for the first four years of study, and health insurance coverage for the first six years of study
  • Gain experience as both a research and teaching assistant, preparing you for roles in academia, health systems, and policy
  • Benefit from close mentorship and collaboration across the University of Rochester Medical Center and the broader health sciences community

STUDENT STORY

Meaningful career experiences. Shaped by mentorship. Rooted in Rochester. 

A passion for improving the lives of cancer survivors and their families led Assistant Professor Meghan Underhill and postdoctoral researcher Yuri Choi '25N (PhD) to Rochester from previous nursing roles in Boston and Seoul. The nurse scientists formed a lasting mentor-advisor connection that has continued to grow through their shared research interests at the NCI-Designated Wilmot Cancer Institute, and the sense of community they have found in a new city. 

Join the next cohort

The PhD in Nursing & Health Science program admits a small, highly selective cohort each fall, fostering close collaboration and individualized support. From day one, you’ll partner with a dedicated faculty mentor, receive guidance in coursework and grant development, and build strong relationships with fellow doctoral students. Together, you’ll engage in a supportive scholarly community designed to help you grow as a researcher and pursue work that makes a meaningful difference in nursing and health science.

Meet the program director

Marie Flannery, PhD, RN

Director, PhD Program in Nursing & Health Science
Professor of Clinical Nursing

Marie Flannery has directed the PhD in Nursing & Health Science program since 2022. She brings extensive clinical experience to her research, which focuses on older adults with advanced cancer and methods to improve symptom reporting and communication between individuals, healthcare providers, and family caregivers to enhance symptom management. Among her honors, she is the recipient of the 2014 Outstanding Oncology Nurse of the Year Award by the March of Dimes, the 2015 Conquer Cancer Foundation Merit Award by the American Society of Clinical Oncology, and the 2022 Cynthia N. Rittenberg Award in Nursing Excellence by Multinational Association for Supportive Care in Cancer.
